WordApp — Support

Help with the app · last updated 13 October 2026

WordApp is a daily devotional: one passage, one reflection, one prayer each morning. If something is not working, or you are unsure how a feature behaves, the answers below cover the questions we get most. Anything else — email us and a person replies.

Contact

Email: support@wordapp.app

Say which iPhone or iPad and iOS version you have, copy the small line at the very bottom of Settings (it names your exact WordApp version and update), and tell us what you saw. We aim to reply within two working days.

Account

How do I delete my account?

Account controls live in Settings.

  1. Open WordApp and tap the lamp at the top right of Home to open You, then the gear at the top right of You (Settings).
  2. Scroll to Data & Account and tap Delete account & data.
  3. Tap Delete on the confirmation.

Your account and every reflection synced to our servers are deleted immediately and permanently. Everything stored on the phone is cleared at the same time and you are signed out.

You do not need to contact us to delete your account — it is done from inside the app. If you cannot find the option, email us and we will delete it for you.

Do I need an account?

No. Tap Skip for now on the sign-in screen and your progress stays on your phone. Signing in adds cloud backup so your streak and reflections survive a new phone — you can sign in later from the completed-day screen (Sign in to sync your progress across devices).

Can I sign in with Apple?

Yes. On the sign-in screen, tap Continue with Apple and confirm with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. If you choose Hide My Email, Apple gives WordApp a private relay address that forwards to your real email. WordApp doesn't keep the name Apple shares.

Translations

Which Bible translations are available?

Three: the King James Version (KJV), the New International Version (NIV) and the Free Bible Version (FBV). Tap the version chip beside any verse (on Home, in the Bible, a devotional or a study) to choose; your choice applies everywhere and is saved on your phone. You can also set it in Settings → Reading. The label under a passage always names the version actually on screen. The English Standard Version and New King James Version are no longer offered; if you had chosen one, WordApp now shows the KJV.

What happens when I'm offline?

A passage you've opened before stays on your phone and shows offline. A passage you've never opened needs a connection the first time; until then, the devotional names the passage so you can read it in a printed Bible. Reconnect and reopen the day to load it.

The daily plan

How is each day's devotional chosen?

WordApp's devotional is a single written sequence of 365 days, walking from Genesis to Revelation — day one, day two, day three — and it is the same for every reader. Nothing is randomised and nothing is personalised: the passage you see on day five is the passage everyone sees on day five. Completing a day moves you to the next written day, so the plan always continues from where you left off, and the verse on the Home screen comes from the same written sequence. The day label at the top of each morning's devotional is your position in the plan, and Home shows it as "Day N of 365" with the section of the Bible you're in.

Streaks

I missed a morning. Is my streak gone?

No. A missed morning never resets your streak. When you come back, WordApp tells you how many mornings you missed and asks, if you'd like to say, what got in the way; you can skip that and go straight to today's passage. Every reflection you wrote is kept.

What counts as completing a day?

Reading the passage and writing a reflection of at least a sentence (20 characters), then tapping Complete. One devotional per calendar day.

Reflections and export

How do I get my reflections out of the app?

On the completed-day screen, tap Export Reflection. The day's passage, reflection question, your response and the prayer are copied to the clipboard as Markdown — paste into Notes, Obsidian, or any journal. Your writing is yours.

Where are my past reflections?

Tap Reflection History on the completed-day screen to read every entry you have written.

Exploring Scripture

Across Scripture

Each devotional day, key moment and Bible figure shows Across Scripture links: where an Old Testament passage is quoted or fulfilled in the New Testament, and where a New Testament passage reaches back into the Old. Each link says whose reading it is: the New Testament's own reading, a later Christian reading, or simply See also. Tap a reference to read it in the Bible.

Key moments

The Bible reader marks each book's key moments, 292 across the 66 books: a small gold "Key moment" label where one starts, a gold rule down its verses, and a one-sentence summary when you tap it.

Great passages and The Law

On Home, tap Learn in Explore, then Great passages: sixteen of Scripture's best-known passages shown as themselves, such as the Ten Commandments, Psalm 23, the Beatitudes and the Lord's Prayer, each with how Christ fulfils it, an application and a Circle question. The Law, in The things of God, sets out what Scripture says about the law and how Christ fulfils it.

The things of God

On Home, tap Learn in Explore, then The things of God: fifteen pages on what Scripture teaches, including prayer, love, forgiveness, faith, the Gospel, the Holy Spirit and baptism. Each has an anchor verse, passages, figures, a devotional and a practice, and where Christians genuinely differ, the page shows the views side by side.

Saved verses

On Home, tap Saved in Explore to see your bookmarks and highlights, and your personal notes on verses under Notes. They stay on your phone.

Circles

The Exodus Road: an eight-session series from the burning bush to the wilderness

On Home, tap Study in Explore, then the 'Through a book' chip, then The Exodus Road. Read it on your own, or start it with your Circle. Each session puts the passage first; Going Deeper then shows what its first hearers heard (with named sources), one Hebrew word to see, how the New Testament reads it, and, where Christians read it differently, each view side by side. Circle leaders and co-leaders also see discussion questions under 'For leaders'.

What is The Exodus Road?
Eight sessions following Israel from the burning bush (Exodus 3) to the edge of the promised land (Numbers 14): the call, the plagues, Passover, the Red Sea, manna, the Ten Commandments, the tabernacle and the wilderness. It is a study plan on the 'Through a book' shelf, and it works alone or with a Circle.

Where does the background in The Exodus Road come from?
From named sources, shown under each session: Hebrew lexicons, commentaries, and ancient texts such as the Laws of Hammurabi. Nothing is written by AI and presented as fact. Where Christians differ, such as the date of the Exodus or who hardened Pharaoh's heart, the views are shown side by side rather than settled for you.

Why can only some people in my Circle see 'For leaders'?
Those discussion questions are for whoever leads the session, so only the Circle's leader and co-leaders see them. Members see the passage, the reflection and Going Deeper as usual.

Circle reading sessions

Open Circles from Home, then your Circle, then today's session to read the passage and answer the study prompts together. Your Circle's answers to a prompt reveal once you've posted your own answer to that same prompt, and you can edit your own answer any time — new answers appear live, so nobody has to leave and come back to see a friend's words.

Why does WordApp ask me to agree before I post in a Circle?
What you write in a Circle can show your faith, which UK law treats as specially protected. So the first time you start or join a Circle, or post in one, WordApp asks whether you agree to it storing what you post and showing it to that Circle. Tap 'I agree' once and you won't be asked again; 'Not now' posts nothing. You can still delete any post, or leave and take your posts with you.

How do I answer a prompt in a Circle session?
Open the session from your Circle, read the passage, then type your answer under a prompt and post it.

Why can't I see what my Circle wrote?
A prompt's answers reveal only after you've posted your own answer to that same prompt — post yours and theirs appear.

Can I change my answer after I've posted it?
Yes — tap your posted answer to edit it at any time.

Notes on a verse, shared with your Circle

While reading a passage in a Circle session, tap a verse (with Word study on, tap its number or hold it) and choose 'Add a note' to write something just about that verse. Only the people in your Circle can see it. Tap the verse again to read every note your Circle has left there.

How do I leave a note on a verse for my Circle?
In a Circle session, tap the verse (with Word study on, tap its number or hold it), choose Add a note, write it, then tap Add note.

Who can see the notes I leave on a verse?
Only the members of that Circle — verse notes are not visible outside your Circle.

Can I report or block someone over a note they left?
Yes — tap the note's author to block or report, the same safety tools as the rest of the Circle.

My notes: your own notes for each Circle session

In a Circle session, under the passage, tap My notes to write a note that is yours alone: what you noticed, what you want to say, what to pray for. It saves on your phone as you type. Every note is listed under My notes at the top of the Circle, one row per session with the Circle's name, the passage, the date and the first line; tap a row to open it. Share this note hands the passage, the date and your note as text to your phone's share sheet. Only you see these notes: they are never sent to WordApp or shown to your Circle. To leave a thought your Circle can read, tap the verse instead.

How do I take my own notes during a Circle session?
Open the session and tap My notes under the passage. Write freely; it saves on your phone as you type. Back returns you to the session.

Who can see My notes?
Only you. A session note stays on your phone and is never sent to WordApp or shown to your Circle. A note on a verse (tap the verse) is the one your Circle sees.

What is the difference between My notes and a note on a verse?
My notes is your own notebook for the session, kept on your phone. A note on a verse is left for your Circle to read. The line under My notes says which is which: Only you see this. To share a thought on a verse, tap the verse.

Where do I find the notes I wrote in earlier sessions?
Open the Circle and tap My notes at the top. Every note on this phone is there, newest first, with the Circle's name, the passage and the date.

Can I share a session note?
Yes. Open the note and tap Share this note, or tap Share on its row in My notes. Your phone's share sheet opens with the passage, the date and your note as text, so you can send it to Notes, a message or anywhere else.

Do my session notes stay if I sign out?
Yes. They belong to this phone, not your account, so signing out keeps them. Deleting your account and data in Settings removes them, and they cannot be got back.

Greek/Hebrew word study in a Circle session

Turn on Word study in Settings, then open a Circle and start a session — any underlined word in the passage can be tapped to see its Greek or Hebrew original, transliteration, and definition, the same as in the Bible reader.

Why can't I tap words in my Circle session for their Greek/Hebrew?
Turn on Word study in Settings first — it's off by default so plain reading stays plain. Once it's on, tap any underlined word in the passage.

Does word study work for every passage in a Circle session?
Yes, for anything in the 66-book canon — the same coverage as the Bible reader.

Circle Gatherings

Open the Gatherings tab inside a Circle to see any planned meet-up. Anyone in the Circle can tap 'Suggest a gathering' to propose one: a meal, a service, a walk, serving together. Tap Yes, Maybe or No to answer. Once people say yes, the leader confirms it. The leader, or whoever suggested it, can cancel it by holding the dots on its card.

How do I RSVP to a Gathering?
Open the Circle's Gatherings tab and tap Yes, Maybe, or No on the card — change your answer any time before it's confirmed.

How do I plan a Gathering for my Circle?
Anyone in the Circle can: open the Gatherings tab, tap Suggest a gathering, give it a title, tap the date to pick it on a calendar and the time to pick it (or leave the time for later), add where, and share it. The leader confirms it once people say yes.

Can members suggest a Gathering, or only the leader?
Anyone in the Circle can suggest one. Only the leader confirms it. The leader or the person who suggested it can cancel it.

Can I get a reminder for a Gathering?
Yes. Once a Gathering is confirmed, if you said Yes or Maybe your phone reminds you at 9am the day before, as long as notifications are on.

Can I find churches or food banks nearby?
In your Circle's Gatherings tab, under Find near you, tap Churches, Charity shops or Food banks. Apple Maps opens, searched for that near where you are.

Your Circles: each Circle as a card

Open Circles from Home. Under Your Circles, each Circle you belong to is a card: its name and plan; where the Circle is in the plan, such as Session 4 of 8, with the passage the next session reads; when it next meets, from the day and time the leader set; what is waiting for you, in words (a prayer request this week, a gathering to answer, an offer to lead, the room live now); and one button to the next step: Continue session, Open the live room while a leader is running one, or Open for a Circle with no session yet. Tap it and the Circle opens on its Sessions tab. If you lead and no time is set, Set a time opens the Circle so you can set When we meet. An offer to lead and any concern to review still sit above the cards. With no Circles yet, the screen says in three lines what a Circle is, then Start a Circle and I have a code.

What does the Circles screen show me?
One card per Circle: its name and plan, where it is in the plan (Session 4 of 8, and the next passage), when it next meets, what is waiting for you in words, and one button that takes you to the next step. Offers to lead and concerns to review sit above the cards.

What does Session 4 of 8 on my Circle's card mean?
Four of the plan's eight sessions have been opened, so the Circle is on the fourth, and the passage after next names what the next session will read. A plan the leader built shows Step instead of Session.

Why does my Circle's card say No time set?
The leader has not set when the Circle meets. If you lead, tap Set a time: the Circle opens, and When we meet takes a day, a time and a note. Every member's card then shows the next meeting.

What does the button on a Circle's card do?
It goes to the next step in one tap. Continue session opens the Circle on its Sessions tab with the latest session at the top; Open the live room shows while a leader is running one; Open is for a Circle with no session yet.

Why does my Circle say No plan yet?
Its leader chose Decide later when starting it. Sessions can still open on any passage. If you lead, open the Circle's Sessions tab to choose a ready-made plan or build one.

Starting or joining a Circle

Open Circles from Home. To start one, tap 'Start a Circle', give it a name, the name you'll use in it, how you usually meet (Remote or In person, each explained on the screen; a leader can change it later), and how the Circle starts its reading: a ready-made plan, one of your own plans, build one now, or decide later. Nothing is chosen for you. Then create it; you lead it. With one of your own plans or 'Build one now', the plan builder opens next; save there to make it the Circle's plan. With 'Decide later' the Circle starts with no plan: its Sessions tab says so, and you can choose or build one any time or open any passage as a session. To join one, tap 'I have a code', enter the invite code someone shared with you and your name, then tap Join. A leader shares the invite from inside the Circle with 'Share the invite', 'Copy code' or the QR code.

How do I start a Circle?
On Home, tap Circles → Start a Circle → give it a name, your name in the Circle, how you meet, and how it starts its reading: a ready-made plan, one of your own plans, build one now, or decide later.

Someone sent me a code. How do I join?
On Home, tap Circles → I have a code → enter the invite code and your name → Join. Codes expire after a while; if yours doesn't work, ask the leader for a fresh one.

How do I invite people to my Circle?
Open the Circle and use Share the invite, Copy code, or let them scan the QR code.

Remote or In person: how a Circle usually meets

When you start a Circle you choose how it usually meets, and the leader can change it any time. Remote is for people in different places or diaries: each of you reads and writes in your own time, and sees the others' answers once you've posted yours. In person is for a Circle in one room: the live room is its main way to meet, where the leader's phone steps everyone through the passage and questions. Any week can be different: a remote Circle that's together can run a session live, and an in-person Circle that's apart reads and writes in its own time. Both include sessions with the passage and questions, Going Deeper, See Him here and a prayer, notes on a verse, the prayer list, Gatherings, the invite and QR code, 'When we meet', and the live room. To change it, the leader or co-leader opens the Circle, taps the Circle tab, and chooses Remote or In person under 'How you usually meet'.

What's the difference between a Remote and an In person Circle?
It's how you usually meet. Remote: everyone reads and writes in their own time. In person: you meet in one room and the leader runs the session live from their phone, while everyone else's phone follows in big type. Either kind can meet the other way any week, and everything else is the same.

Can I change my Circle from Remote to In person?
Yes, any time. If you lead or co-lead the Circle, open it, tap the Circle tab, and choose Remote or In person under 'How you usually meet'. Everyone's phone shows 'Now meeting in person' (or remotely) on the Circle for a week. Meeting the other way for just one week? You don't need to switch: any session can be run live when you're together.

We usually meet remotely but we're together this week. Can we use the live room?
Yes. Open the session, then in the Sessions tab the leader taps 'Meeting in person this time? Run it live'. Everyone else taps the gold 'Live now' bar.

Can an In person Circle still write answers during the week?
Yes. Every session works the same in both: anyone can read the passage and write their answer in their own time, before or after you meet.

The live room

Any Circle can run a session live when you're together. The leader opens a session, then taps 'Run this session live in the room' (in a Circle that usually meets remotely, it's the smaller link 'Meeting in person this time? Run it live'). Their phone becomes the remote: Passage, then each Prompt, then 'Phones down — talk', which dims every screen so the table can talk. Everyone else taps the gold 'Live now' bar in the Circle, and their phone follows in big type. The leader taps End the session when you're done; everything written stays in the Circle.

How do I join the live room?
Open the Circle. When your leader has gone live, a gold 'Live now — join the room' bar appears at the top. Tap it and your phone follows along.

How do I run a session live as the leader?
Open a session first, then in the Sessions tab tap 'Run this session live in the room' (or 'Meeting in person this time? Run it live' in a Circle that usually meets remotely). Step through Passage and each Prompt; tap 'Phones down — talk' when it's time to talk.

My screen went dark in the live room. What happened?
The leader chose Phones down, so the table can talk. The screen comes back when they bring phones back. To leave, hold the dark screen.

Why don't I see the live room in my Circle?
Every Circle has it, but a leader has to open a session and go live first. Only the leader or co-leader can start it; then a gold 'Live now' bar appears for everyone else.

A Circle's settings, explained

Open your Circle and tap the Circle tab, then 'This Circle's settings, explained'. Each setting says what it does and who sees it: how you usually meet (the leaders switch Remote or In person right there), When we meet (the leaders set it; each phone reminds its owner if notifications are on), the invite (anyone can share it; leaders can make a new code), leaders and co-leaders, private prayer, blocking and reporting, and leaving. Companion walks you through it the first time; tap How this works to see it again.

Where are my Circle's settings?
Open the Circle, tap the Circle tab, then 'This Circle's settings, explained'. The leader changes When we meet by tapping its card, and the invite from Invite at the top right.

How do I set when our Circle meets?
If you lead, tap the When we meet card, choose a day, then tap the time to pick it: an hour and minutes, read back in words such as 7:30 pm. Add a note if you like, and tap Save the rhythm. Everyone sees it, and each phone reminds its owner if notifications are on.

How do I see Companion's Circle guide again?
Open the Circle and tap How this works, just under the Circle's name. Companion's full welcome shows again.

Prayer requests in your Circle

Open your Circle and tap the Prayer tab. Type into 'A request for the Circle' and tap Add. Members tap 'I prayed' when they have. When it's answered, the person who asked taps 'answered' and can add how. Tick 'Only the leaders can see this' for something you'd rather keep between you and the Circle's leaders.

How do I share a prayer request?
Open your Circle → Prayer tab → type your request → Add.

Who can see what I share in a Circle?
Only the members of that Circle. A request marked 'Only the leaders can see this' is seen by the Circle's leaders only. Nothing you share in a Circle is shown to other WordApp users.

Custom study plans for your Circle

As the Circle's leader, open the Sessions tab and scroll to Study Plans, then tap '+ Build a plan'. It opens the same plan builder as Study's 'Build your own plan': pick books and chapters, paste a list, start from a ready-made plan, or bring one of your own plans with 'From my plans'. Each passage is one session; add your own question under any passage, take the one WordApp suggests where it has one, or leave it and the usual questions show. Choose the session days, then tap 'Save and use in the Circle' — it becomes the Circle's plan, and 'Open next step' opens its sessions in order. 'Edit plan' changes it later; sessions already opened stay as they are. A Circle started on a ready-made plan shows it in Study Plans as the active plan, with how many sessions are open and a button for the next one; 'Open a session' names the plan (Start The Twelve — session 1, then Continue), and each session in the list says which plan it belongs to.

How do I create a study plan for my Circle?
As the leader, open the Sessions tab, scroll to Study Plans and tap '+ Build a plan'. Add passages, set the session days, then tap 'Save and use in the Circle'.

Can I use one of my own reading plans in my Circle?
Yes. In '+ Build a plan', tap 'From my plans' and choose one: its passages become the Circle's sessions, one each. Your own plan, its progress and your journal stay on your phone.

How do I add my own question to a session?
Under each passage in the builder there's a box for your question (up to 300 characters). Members see it when that session opens. Where WordApp already has a question written for that passage, it shows under the box: tap 'Use this question' to take it (you can still change it) or 'Not this one' to hide it. Leave the box empty and the usual questions show.

How do I change a Circle plan?
Tap 'Edit plan' on it. You can add, remove or reorder the passages still to come and change their questions; sessions already opened stay as they are.

How do I make my Circle start a plan I built?
Saving a plan makes it the Circle's plan. For an older plan, tap Activate. 'Open next step' opens its steps as sessions in order.

Can WordApp suggest a discussion question for my Circle?
Yes. When you build a Circle plan, a question already written for that passage shows under the box. Tap Use this question to take it, or Not this one to hide it.

Can my Circle go back to the plan it started with?
Yes. The ready-made plan stays in Study Plans after you build one of your own. Tap Activate on it to make it the Circle's plan again. Sessions already opened stay as they are.

Leaving a Circle, and reporting or blocking a member

Open your Circle, tap a member's name to block or report them, or tap 'Leave this Circle' at the bottom to leave. Blocking hides that person's words from you; reporting tells us what happened so we can look into it. Leaving is immediate and can't be undone from inside the app — ask to be re-invited if you change your mind.

How do I leave a Circle?
Open the Circle, scroll to the bottom, and tap Leave this Circle.

How do I report someone in my Circle?
Tap their name, choose Report, describe what happened, and we'll look into it.

What does blocking someone in a Circle do?
It hides their words from you everywhere in that Circle, without removing them or telling them you blocked them.

Delete something you posted in a Circle

You can delete your own answer, prayer request or verse note in a Circle from the post itself. On your answer in a session, tap 'delete' next to 'edit'. On your prayer request, in the Prayer tab, tap 'delete'. On your verse note, open the verse's notes and tap 'delete' on yours. Each time you're asked to confirm, and once it's deleted your Circle won't see it again. To leave a Circle and delete everything you posted there in one go, open the Members tab and tap 'Leave and remove my posts' at the bottom.

How do I delete an answer I posted in my Circle?
Open the session, find your answer (marked YOU), tap delete and confirm. You can write a new one any time.

How do I delete a prayer request?
Open your Circle, go to the Prayer tab, tap delete on your request and confirm.

How do I delete a note I left on a verse?
In the session, tap the verse's note marker to open its notes, tap delete on yours and confirm.

Can I leave a Circle and take my posts with me?
Yes. Open the Members tab, tap 'Leave and remove my posts' at the bottom and confirm. Your answers, prayer requests and verse notes in that Circle are deleted and you leave it. Your posts in other Circles stay.

Can I delete someone else's post?
No, only your own. If someone's post worries you, tap it to report it or block them.

What happens to a report if the post is deleted?
The report stays with the people who review it, so a concern is still looked at. The post's words are gone.

Reading and listening

Learn — Bible Figures and Honest Questions

On Home, tap Learn in Explore to open a list of study shelves — The Life of Jesus, Bible Figures, The things of God, Words that drive doctrine and Honest Questions, with more added the same way over time. Tap a shelf to open it; the back arrow (or a swipe back) returns you to Learn.

Where did Bible Figures go?
It moved to Learn — on Home, tap Learn in Explore, then tap Bible Figures.

Where did Faith & Reason go?
It's now called Honest Questions, and it lives in Learn — on Home, tap Learn in Explore, then tap Honest Questions.

What is the Learn hub?
One place for every study shelf — Bible Figures, Honest Questions, and more as they're added — instead of each one having its own row on Home.

Where does a Bible Figures profile's information come from?
Every profile ends with a Sources section: the Scripture passages first, then the reference works, with each reference work's link tappable so you can check it yourself.

Where do the Honest Questions profiles get their facts?
Every profile ends with Their Work & Sources: the person's own books first, then scholarly works about them, so you can check each one yourself.

Can I tap a Bible reference to read it?
Yes. Tap any reference in WordApp, such as John 3:16, and the verse opens right where you are. Tap Open in Bible to read the whole chapter; Back brings you to where you were.

Text size

On Home, tap the lamp at the top right to open You, then the gear at the top right of You. Under Reading, pick Small, Default, Large or Largest under Text size. It applies to the devotional, the Bible and Circle sessions.

How do I make the text bigger?
Settings → Reading → Text size — choose Large or Largest.

Where do I change the font size?
Settings → Reading → Text size.

Does text size affect the whole app?
It applies everywhere you read: the devotional, the Bible, and Circle sessions.

Listen to today's devotional

On the Home screen, under today's devotional, tap 'Listen' to have the passage and devotional read aloud; tap it again to stop. If you don't see it, turn it on in Settings → Reading → 'Read aloud control on the devotional'.

Can WordApp read the devotional out loud to me?
Yes — tap Listen under today's devotional on Home. Turn it on first in Settings → Reading if it's not showing.

Where is the listen button?
Under the passage on Home, once 'Read aloud control' is switched on in Settings → Reading.

How do I stop the reading?
Tap the same button again — it changes to Stop while it's speaking.

Siri, widget and reminders

Ask Siri for today's verse

Say 'Hey Siri, what's today's verse in WordApp?' and Siri speaks the verse of the day without opening the app. 'Hey Siri, what's my streak in WordApp?' and 'Hey Siri, begin today's devotional in WordApp' work too. Say WordApp as one word; if Siri opens another app, add the action from the Shortcuts app instead. On WordApp 1.4.0 or later, 'Hey Siri, get today's verse from WordApp' works too.

Can I ask Siri for the verse of the day?
Yes. Say 'Hey Siri, what's today's verse in WordApp?'

How do I set up the WordApp Siri shortcut?
Open the Shortcuts app, add the 'Get Today's Verse' action from WordApp, and record a phrase if you want a custom one.

Home Screen and Lock Screen widget

Open WordApp once first, so the widget has something to show. Then long-press an empty part of your Home Screen until the icons jiggle, tap Edit, then Add Widget, search for WordApp, pick the small or medium size and tap Add Widget. It shows your streak, today's day and the verse of the day, and updates when you complete a day.

How do I add the WordApp widget to my Home Screen?
Long-press an empty part of your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, search for WordApp, choose a size and tap Add Widget. Open WordApp once first so it has something to show.

Can I put WordApp on my Lock Screen?
Yes. Long-press your Lock Screen, tap Customise, pick the Lock Screen, tap the widget row under the clock and choose WordApp Streak.

Why is my WordApp widget blank, or why can't I find it?
Open WordApp once, then look again. If WordApp still isn't in the widget list, check you have the latest version from the App Store; the widget needs version 1.3.2 or later.

Morning reminder

On Home, tap the lamp at the top right to open You, then the gear at the top right of You. Under Reminders, turn on Daily reminder, then tap the time to pick it. Reminders are local to your phone — nothing about your reading is sent anywhere. If it doesn't fire, check iPhone Settings → WordApp → Notifications → Allow Notifications.

How do I set a morning reminder?
Settings → Reminders → turn on Daily reminder, then tap the time to pick it, to the minute, such as 7:30 am.

My reminder isn't firing — what do I check?
iPhone Settings → WordApp → Notifications → Allow Notifications, then check the time under Settings → Reminders in WordApp.

Where did the reminder setting move to?
It's in WordApp's Settings now, under Reminders — it is no longer on the completed-day screen.

Can each of my plans remind me?
Yes. Open My plans, tap Edit on the plan, then Remind me under Reminder. The Daily reminder in Settings is for today's devotional.

The Life of Jesus

On Home, tap Learn in Explore, then The Life of Jesus, first on the list. Choose His Ministry (his life in order), The Twelve, The Sermon on the Mount, His Miracles, His Parables or His Teachings. Each entry says what the text says, lists the passages (every Gospel that tells it), shows Across Scripture back into the Old Testament, and ends with one application for today. Tap any reference to read it in the Bible. The study plan Christ in all the Scriptures sits at the bottom of the shelf.

Where can I read about the life of Jesus?
In Learn: tap Learn on Home, then The Life of Jesus. His ministry is told in order, from his birth to the ascension.

Where are the miracles and parables of Jesus?
In Learn, The Life of Jesus: His Miracles lists every miracle the Gospels record, and His Parables every parable, with his own explanation where he gives one.

Who were the twelve disciples?
In Learn, The Life of Jesus, The Twelve: each apostle from what Scripture itself says of him, with the reference for every fact. Where Scripture records little beyond a name, it says so.

What does Across Scripture mean?
It traces a passage through the rest of the Bible: where an Old Testament passage is quoted or fulfilled in the New Testament, and where a New Testament passage reaches back into the Old. Each link says whose reading it is: the New Testament's own reading, a later Christian reading, or simply See also.

Study plans: Follow Jesus, then tracks

On Home, tap Your path, or Study in Explore. Follow Jesus comes first, in three stages: Meet him (Knowing Jesus), Walk with him (the Life of Jesus series: The Twelve, The Sermon on the Mount, Miracles of Jesus, Parables of Jesus and the 'I am' sayings), and See him everywhere (Christ in all the Scriptures, or its short version, Prophecy Fulfilled). Below it are the tracks, each with a line saying what it holds: Life and struggle, Questions, Together, and Through a book (one book of the Bible read as a story, starting with The Exodus Road). The chips under the title show one shelf at a time. Study plans opens on the shelf for your path, and All shelves brings every shelf back. Every plan says how many sessions it has and whether it is Ready for Circles. 'Build your own plan' is the first row, then the plans suggested for you, from what you told us when you signed up. Every plan is open to everyone, in any order.

Where do I start with the study plans?
With Follow Jesus, at the top of Study plans: Meet him with Knowing Jesus, walk with him through the Life of Jesus series, then see him everywhere in Christ in all the Scriptures. Nothing is locked, so you can start anywhere.

What happened to Themes?
Themes are now tracks. Brothers in Christ is in the Together track and Defending the Faith is in Questions, beside Science & Faith. Nothing was removed, and Circles already on those plans carry on as before.

What's the difference between Defending the Faith and Science & Faith?
Both are in Questions. Defending the Faith takes one hard question a session (suffering, God's silence, trusting the Bible, science, the resurrection, hypocrisy, unanswered prayer, doubt): the strongest form of the objection, the answer from Scripture and reason, and what stays hard. Science & Faith follows eleven scientists and thinkers, one a session (Kepler, Pascal, Newton, Faraday, Lemaître and others): their faith, and the strongest case against it.

Is a plan in Through a book different from a study plan?
No. Every plan on every shelf is a study plan with sessions, and works the same way alone or in a Circle. Through a book is just the shelf for plans that follow one book of the Bible as a story.

Where did the long list of plans go?
It's still there. Tap All shelves at the start of the chips to see every shelf at once, or tap a shelf to see just its plans.

What does Ready for Circles mean?
Every session in that plan has its own discussion questions for a Circle, so a group can study it together. Pick it when you create a Circle.

What is the short version of Christ in all the Scriptures?
Prophecy Fulfilled: six sessions on prophecies the New Testament says Jesus fulfilled. Christ in all the Scriptures is the full seventeen, from Genesis to Luke 24.

Where is the plan I built?
On Home, under Your path. Once you have a plan of your own it shows there first, with its title, where you are in it (such as 'Today: Day 3') and today's passages, and one tap opens that day. It is also under Study, then 'My plans', with your journal beside it. With more than one plan, Home shows the first one still going, and 'and 1 more in My plans' opens the list.

My own plan: build or paste a study plan, with a journal

On Home, tap Study in Explore, then 'Build your own plan', the first row at the top of Study Plans. Pick a book and its chapters (or the whole book, or only some verses of one chapter), or tap 'Paste a list' and paste a plan you already follow, such as 'Genesis 1-3, Matthew 1; Psalm 23', then tap 'Read the list'. WordApp shows what it understood and what it didn't, with the reason, and adds only what was understood. Choose chapters or verses a day, tap any day of the week to make it a rest day, pick when to start (Today, Tomorrow, Next Monday, a day either side, or Pick a date for a calendar), and the preview says how many days it takes and when it ends. Each day shows the passage in your Bible version, then what WordApp already has on it (key moments, See Him here, the people in it, the book's introduction, word studies, Great passages, the things of God, questions to check what you read), then questions to reflect on, a prayer prompt and a journal. Your plans are under 'My plans', with the Journal beside them, and once you have one it shows first on Home under Your path, with today's passages and one tap to the day.

How do I make my own reading plan?
On Home, tap Study in Explore, then 'Build your own plan' at the top of Study Plans. Pick chapters or paste a list, set the pace, and save. If you'd rather be asked, tap 'Set it up with Companion' instead.

Can Companion help me set up a plan?
Yes. On Study Plans, tap 'Set it up with Companion' (it shows until you have a plan of your own), or open Companion and tap 'Set up a reading plan, step by step', or tap the line under the guide in 'Build your own plan'. Companion asks one thing at a time: what to read (books, a list you paste, or a ready-made plan), how many chapters a day, which days you read, when to start, the order, and whether you'd like a reminder. Then it shows the whole plan to check, and 'Create my plan' opens Day 1. Companion's questions are fixed ones written for the app; it doesn't write anything about your passages.

Can I start from a ready-made plan?
Yes. In 'Build your own plan', tap 'Start from a plan', or choose 'Start from a ready-made plan' when Companion sets it up with you. There are two: New Testament in 90 days, and the Bible in a year (in Bible order, Genesis to Revelation; it isn't a chronological plan). Each sets a pace you can change before you save.

Can I get a reminder for my plan?
Yes. In 'Build your own plan' (or 'Edit' on a plan in My plans), under Reminder, tap 'Remind me' and choose a time. Companion asks the same when it sets up a plan with you. It comes on your reading days only, never on a rest day, says the plan's name and that today's reading is ready, and stops when every day is read. Tapping it opens the day you're on. If notifications are off for WordApp, turn them on in your iPhone's Settings, then WordApp, then Notifications.

Can I search or export my journal?
Yes. Open 'My plans' and tap 'Journal'. Type in the search box to find entries by what you wrote or by passage. 'Export' sends the whole journal, grouped by plan and date, to the share sheet, so you can save it to Notes or Files or send it to yourself.

Can I use the reading plan I already follow?
Yes. In 'Build your own plan', tap 'Paste a list' and paste it, for example 'Genesis 1-3, Matthew 1; Psalm 23'. WordApp shows each reference it understood and any it didn't, with the reason, so nothing goes missing without you knowing.

Does WordApp write commentary on my passages?
No. Each day shows only what the app already has on those verses (key moments, links through Scripture, Bible figures, the book's introduction, word studies, Great passages, the things of God and study questions), and says plainly when it has nothing. Nothing is written by AI.

How do I count a day as read?
At the foot of the day, under the journal, tap 'Mark this day as read'. It turns to 'Read' with a tick, and My plans, Study Plans and Home move on to the next day straight away. Tap it again to mark the day not read.

Can I read two days in one day, or start the next day early?
Yes. A plan goes at your pace. Once a day is marked read, the next one is ready to open: tap 'Day 2 →' at the foot of the day, or open the plan again from Home or My plans. The dates are only a guide; reading ahead never breaks anything, and there are no streaks.

What happens if I miss a day?
Nothing bad. There are no streaks. The plan simply picks up where you left off, and you can change the pace at any time; days you have read stay as they are and only the rest are laid out again.

Where is my journal kept?
Only on this phone. It isn't sent anywhere or shared with your Circle. Open 'My plans' and tap 'Journal' to read every entry by plan and date. Deleting a plan keeps what you wrote.

Will signing out delete my journal?
No. Your plans and journal stay on this phone when you sign out, ready when you sign back in. They are removed only if you delete your account, or delete the app.

How do I change the pace or the passages of my plan?
Open 'My plans' and tap 'Edit' on the plan. Change chapters or verses a day, the reading days, or the passages still to read, then tap 'Save changes'.

Can my plan start on a date weeks away?
Yes. Under Start, tap Pick a date and choose the day on the calendar. Today, Tomorrow and Next Monday stay one tap, and the arrows still move a day either side.

Can I delete several of my plans at once?
Yes. Open 'My plans', tap 'Select', check the plans you want gone, then tap 'Delete selected'. One confirm removes all of them, journal entries included. Deleting one plan the usual way still keeps its journal.

Something is not working

  • The passage does not load: check your connection. A passage you have opened before shows offline; one you haven't needs a connection the first time. Force-quit and reopen the app.
  • The widget shows 0 or yesterday's verse: open the app once to push fresh data to the widget. If it persists after completing today's devotional, remove and re-add the widget.
  • The app crashes or freezes: update to the latest version in the App Store, then reopen. If it persists, email us with your iOS and WordApp versions.
  • Sign-in fails: check the email address; the confirmation email can land in spam. Forgot your password? Tap Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen. Skip for now still lets you read today.
  • I can't find my Circle: confirm you entered the invite code exactly as shared. Codes are case-sensitive. Ask your Circle leader to resend it.
